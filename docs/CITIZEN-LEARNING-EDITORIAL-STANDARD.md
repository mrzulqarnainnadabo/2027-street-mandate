# Civic Mandate Nigeria — Citizen Learning Editorial Standard

## Purpose
Civic Mandate Nigeria is first a citizen-understanding and public-participation platform. Recording public-service demands is one part of the system, not its entire purpose.

The public experience follows this pathway:

**Know → Understand → Participate → Observe → Follow through**

## Constitutional source hierarchy
- Use the Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 as amended as the primary legal source.
- Rights summaries must identify the relevant section and distinguish provisions applying to “every person” from those expressly applying to “citizens”.
- Section 24 duties must be described as constitutional duties; they do not cancel fundamental rights or authorise unlawful action.
- Section 23 national ethics may inform civic learning, but must not be used as a partisan loyalty test.
- Section 45 restrictions and Section 46 enforcement routes must be acknowledged where relevant.
- A short explainer is not legal advice. Link the original text and encourage qualified legal support for individual cases.

## ISEYC framing of patriotism
ISEYC describes patriotism as informed, peaceful and useful citizenship:
- Respect the Constitution and the rights of other people.
- Contribute positively to community well-being.
- Protect public and shared resources.
- Participate peacefully in public life.
- Ask public institutions to account for their lawful responsibilities.
- Reject corruption, discrimination, violence and misinformation.

Patriotism is not silence, party loyalty, government endorsement or uncritical support for whoever holds office.

## Learning experience
Prefer short visual modules over long essays:
1. Rights and protections.
2. Responsibilities and national ethics.
3. Federal, state and local government.
4. Executive, legislature and judiciary.
5. Elections and lawful participation.
6. Budgets, procurement and public services.
7. Community observation and evidence literacy.
8. Following public commitments without confusing announcement, implementation and verified outcome.

Each module should include:
- One clear learning objective.
- A small set of visual points.
- Relevant constitutional section or official source.
- “What this does not mean” where misinterpretation is likely.
- One practical, lawful next step.

## Evidence and neutrality
- Separate constitutional text, official guidance, public statistics, community observations, analysis and recommendations.
- Never imply that a public submission is representative of all Nigerians.
- Never describe an announcement as implementation or a recommendation as action taken.
- Do not publish candidate rankings, endorsements, party comparisons or electoral persuasion.
- Do not equate the absence of a report with the absence of a problem.
- Keep public education accessible, low-bandwidth and readable on a 360px phone.

## Review rule
Legal and civic learning content must be checked against the current authoritative text before publication. Record source version, review date and reviewer. Where the current amendment status is uncertain, disclose it and link to the official source rather than presenting a simplified claim as settled law.
